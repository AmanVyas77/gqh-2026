Feedlot Margins, Cattle Prices and Beef-Exposed Equities: Two Pre-Registered Tests

Gator Quant Hacks 2026 · Systematic Trading Track · [team names] · Research note; holdout evaluated once on 2026-10-04

Summary. We pre-registered two hypotheses linking cattle markets to tradable returns, tested each frozen primary specification on development data (2010–2024-09), and then evaluated both once on a two-year holdout (2024-10-01 to 2026-10-02).

  • H1 (main study): deeply negative feedlot margins did not predict fewer cattle placements (P1: β = +0.0020 per s.d., Newey-West t = 0.42), and the primary trading rule earned a net Sharpe ratio of −0.08 (P2: mean monthly net return −0.045%, t = −0.34). By the pre-registered decision table, H1 is rejected for this specification.
  • H2 (follow-up): Tyson and Texas Roadhouse, hedged with their sector ETFs, showed no same-month cattle exposure (Q1: t = +0.10) and no lagged reaction (Q2: t = +0.88); the primary rule earned a net Sharpe ratio of −0.29. H2 is rejected for this specification.
  • Across the 25 cattle-project trials, full-sample development net Sharpe ratios range from −0.42 to +0.21; no monthly-return t-statistic exceeds 0.86.
  • Holdout: H1’s primary earned a net Sharpe ratio of +0.63 (t = 0.97), meeting P2’s holdout condition, but P2 also required in-sample significance and P1 had already failed, so the decision table still rejects H1. H2’s primary earned −0.15 and fails Q3.

These results show no support for the tested specifications. They are not evidence that every cattle-related strategy is unprofitable.

1. Economic mechanisms and hypotheses

H1: feeding margins and Live Cattle futures. A feedlot buys an 800 lb feeder steer, feeds it about 64 bushels of corn and sells it at 1,400 lb roughly five months later. The projected margin is Mt = 1,400 × LEt − 800 × FCt − 64.29 × Ct − $350, using CME settlements of a Live Cattle contract expiring at least 150 days out, the nearest Feeder Cattle contract with 30 days left and a corn contract at least 75 days out. Two channels could make low margins bullish for deferred cattle futures: low margins cut placements, so fewer fed cattle reach market five to six months later; and hedging feedlots pay speculators a premium to carry price risk (Keynes 1930; de Roon, Nijman & Veld 2000). The signal is zt, the margin's distance from its mean over the 36 prior month-ends in standard deviations (the $350 non-corn cost cancels). Tests: P1 low z predicts fewer placements over months t+1..t+3; P2 the primary rule (Variant A: w = clip(−z/2, −1, 1) × 10% / σ̂, |w| ≤ 2, long or short the deferred contract) earns a positive in-sample net return with t > 2 and a positive holdout Sharpe; P3 the kill test asks whether z predicts the realized spot change but not the futures return, meaning the curve already prices the supply response. Variants B (the full margin spread) and C (A filtered by commercial hedging pressure) are pre-registered secondary rules.

H2: cattle prices and beef-buying equities. Cattle are a major input for Tyson (beef segment) and Texas Roadhouse (largest commodity cost). If equity investors underreact to cost news that originates in a separate market (Cohen & Frazzini 2008; Menzly & Ozbas 2010), a rise in cattle prices this month should predict sector-relative underperformance next month. The signal is st, the month's front Live Cattle return divided by its standard deviation over the 36 prior months. Each stock is hedged with its sector ETF (XLP, XLY) at a 252-day beta; the position is w = −clip(s/2, −1, 1) × 10% / σ̂basket. Tests: Q1 same-month exposure (β < 0, t < −2); Q2 next-month predictability (β < 0, t < −2); Q3 positive net returns with t > 2 in-sample and a positive holdout Sharpe.

Timeline. H1 was committed on 2026-10-02 before any backtest; its deviation log has 21 dated entries, each recorded before the results it affects. H2 was conceived after H1 failed and committed on 2026-10-03 before any equity data was loaded; its implementation choices were logged before fitting.

Why it failed. When z < −1 (15 month-ends), the margin recovered by +$153 per head over five months, almost entirely through the Live Cattle leg (+$156). But that leg compares different contract months at t and t+5; P3 shows that neither the held contract's return nor the realized spot change was predictable from z. A pre-declared diagnostic finds that the calendar-seasonal component of the margin alone explains 74% of Variant A's monthly return variance over 2018–2024 (81% for C), while an LE-price-only version explains 4%. An exploratory diagnostic (not a test) found that the LE-price z alone carried a negative association with placements (t = −3.31), consistent with the cattle cycle; no strategy was built on it.

5. Robustness, uncertainty, limitations and conclusion

Figure 3. H1 one-at-a-time robustness (21 alternative specifications plus the 3 primaries), net Sharpe on the common window from 2014-07-01; the seasonal-adjustment specification can only start in 2018-07.

Robustness. Changing the z lookback (24, 48 months), sale horizon (120, 180 days), feed conversion (5.5, 6.5) or seasonal adjustment moved full-sample net Sharpe ratios between −0.42 and +0.21 (up to +0.22 on the common window), with no monthly t-statistic above 0.86. The overlay and 2× costs leave the conclusion unchanged.

The calculation covers the 25 distinct cattle-project trials (24 for H1, 1 for H2; expected maximum Sharpe under the null 0.31 a year). It does not cover other experiments in the team's repository, which also holds separate GLP-1 equity, equity pairs-trading and macro-equity studies; those are disclosed here but not counted. Other checks: regressing Variant A on long-only cattle, 12-month cattle momentum and SPY gives alpha −1.0% a year (t = −0.64, R² = 0.08). Stress: Variant A lost 5.5% in the March–May 2020 packer shutdowns and 1.4% around the August 2019 Holcomb plant fire; H2 lost 9.9% in March–May 2020. Two consecutive Live Cattle limit moves (8.50 then 12.75 cents/lb) at the 2× leverage cap would cost 22.9% of capital. Variant A's median position was 0.11% of 20-day volume and 1.9% initial margin-to-equity; under the square-root impact model, net Sharpe falls from −0.11 at $1M to −0.95 at $1B.

Limitations. Small samples (15 month-ends with z < −1; 134 monthly H2 signals); feed gaps and missing final settlements in the futures data; inferred final settlements before 2017; revised placement data; current exchange margins applied historically; a two-stock H2 universe exposed to idiosyncratic events and survivorship. Not completed: H2's four registered alternative trials, its Fama-French factor regression and its liquidity analysis; CME exchange-set position limits for LE; a whole-pipeline perturbation test for look-ahead; an Iowa State closeout comparison of the margin's level. None of these is implied to have passed.

Conclusion. Neither pre-registered mechanism found support in the tested specifications: feedlot margins did not forecast placements or futures returns in development, and beef-exposed equities did not respond to cattle prices with a lag. H1’s positive two-year holdout does not change the pre-registered verdict (Section 6). We report this as a negative result. It does not establish that no cattle-related strategy is profitable.

Interpretation. H1’s primary was profitable in the holdout, and its holdout Sharpe ratio meets P2’s out-of-sample condition. Two years is a short sample: the standard error of an annualized Sharpe ratio over two years is about 0.7, so +0.63 is not statistically distinguishable from zero (monthly t = 0.97) or from the development estimate of −0.08. P2 required both conditions, and its in-sample condition failed; P1, the mechanism test, had failed before any trading result was seen. Under the pre-registered decision table H1 remains rejected. The holdout result is reported as observed. H2’s primary lost money in the holdout and fails Q3. Variants B and C were also positive in the holdout; they are pre-registered secondary rules and are not substituted for the primary. With the drawdown overlay, B and C stayed at half size throughout the holdout (their overlaid runs’ own drawdowns, carried over from development, stayed above the 7.5% restore threshold at every holdout month-end), so their overlay Sharpe ratios equal the unscaled ones.
